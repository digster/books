The joint map

Decompose the claim into the conditions it rests on. Number them, because everything after this refers to them by number, and give each one a stable id so prose elsewhere can link straight to joint 3 rather than asking the reader to scroll and count.1

  1. The first condition

    State the condition in the book's terms, then spend a paragraph on whatever the author assumed you would already know. This is where a readthrough genuinely beats the book: the book says as so-and-so showed and moves on; the page stops and explains what so-and-so showed.

  2. The second condition

    Keep the particulars at full resolution inside each joint. A joint stated abstractly is a joint the reader cannot test against a case, and testing against cases is the entire point of numbering them.

  3. The third condition

    Where the book is more confident than its evidence — or less — note it, but note it as attribution inside the readthrough and put the assessment itself in the notes section. The reader must always be able to tell whose sentence they are reading.

  4. The fourth condition

    Books almost never say whether their conditions are conjunctive or disjunctive, and the interesting failure usually lives exactly there.

The joints are conjunctive, and joint 4 is carrying the argument. Naming the load-bearing joint — the one whose removal changes the outcome most — is what separates the book's catastrophes from the ordinary background functioning of the same forces.

A named case, joint by joint

Take one of the book's own cases and run it through the joints in order, showing which are present, in what degree, and how the outcome followed. Every step shown, nothing skipped as obvious. A case narrated as a story is not a case walked — the difference is whether the reader can see the argument's machinery engaging with the particulars.

  1. Joint 1 is present. Show it with the specifics — a place, a year, a quantity such as 1,240 households, a named actor. The numbered steps are set in the machine face because a walk is machinery being operated, not prose being read.
  2. Joint 2 is present, in degree. Degree matters: a joint half-present predicts a different outcome than a joint fully present, and saying which is what makes the walk testable.
  3. Joint 3 converts the necessity into a good. Quote sparingly here — under fifteen words, exact, attributed — and only where the book's own phrasing is itself the evidence.
  4. Joint 4 removes the correcting feedback. The outcome follows from the structure rather than being asserted alongside it.

A case that runs 1–3 and stops

Mandatory, because without it the joints look like a single undifferentiated force. Find a case that satisfies all the joints but the load-bearing one and show what the claim then predicts — and whether the record agrees. This is the contrast test from the five tests, run on the page rather than left to the reader.

Note

This is the site speaking, and it is set into the page rather than on it so that you can tell at a glance. Evaluation lives here and in the afterword — nowhere else. It has to be specific: some critics have questioned this is worthless. Who, on what grounds, and what did the book's defenders say back?

A long stretch of unbroken prose follows, because a stylesheet that only ever gets tested against short blocks is a stylesheet that has not been tested. Write from understanding, never from the page. Build the model, close the book, write. Sentence-by-sentence work against the source produces paraphrase, which is both worse reading and a legal problem — if your paragraphs map onto the author's paragraphs, you have paraphrased rather than retold. Retelling reorganises, connects, and explains what the author assumed you would infer, which is why a page can be shorter than the chapter it covers and still transmit more of it.

The prose stays continuous. Bullets are for genuinely enumerable things, and a page that is mostly bullets is a summary wearing a costume. The failure this site exists to avoid is the one where understanding gets compressed into its own residue — the takeaways — and the residue gets distributed as though it were the thing. A summary tells you what a book is about; this tells you what is in it, which is roughly the difference between a menu and a meal.2

Density is not coverage. A part that mentions eleven case studies in a list has covered none of them, and the moment a page says the author gives several examples it has deleted the book. If the book spends nine pages on a case, the page spends real space on that case. The rule is blunt because the failure is so common.

Quotes are short, exact, attributed, and rare.

The support ledger

The cost-sheet analogue for a claim. Per joint: what evidence is actually offered, of what type, and how much weight it can bear.

3Inference from the theory, not independently evidencedinferenceAsserted, not established — see the ledger below

Then the two things claim books systematically leave implicit. How the cases were selected: a book that examines only failures has selected on the dependent variable, and the reader should know that before deciding what the cases prove. Saying so is not an attack; it is a specification of what the evidence can and cannot establish. What the claim buys and pays: explanatory reach is purchased with something, usually elasticity — a frame that can be fitted to any outcome after the fact explains a great deal and predicts nothing.

Voice

Short. How the book itself handles this material — framing, register, recurring metaphors, characteristic moves. Knowing how a book feels is part of having read it. Omit this section entirely where there is nothing distinctive to say; do not pad it.

What this establishes, and what it asserts

The separation that keeps a reader honest. What did this part actually establish, and what did it merely assert? Where is the prose more confident than the evidence, and where is it less? For a claim part, this is also where the case selection gets stated plainly.

Notes

The site's assessment, boxed and labelled so it can never be mistaken for transmission. Cover, where relevant: the replication status of cited studies; whether a described system still works that way; contested history; what the field concluded afterward; and the strongest objection the book does not answer.

Fairness is a requirement, not a courtesy. For contested material, present the dispute rather than adjudicating it, and give the book's side its best form rather than its most convenient one.
