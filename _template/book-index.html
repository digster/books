Book Title

Orientation

What kind of book this is and what it is trying to do. What world it came out of — the argument it was written against, the discipline it was addressed to, what was assumed common knowledge at the time and no longer is. What a reader needs before starting, stated honestly: if the book assumes a field's vocabulary, say which, and supply it here rather than leaving the reader to discover the gap four parts in.

Also: which edition these pages follow, since chapter numbering and sometimes content differ between them.

The arc

How the exposition actually moves, start to finish, in a few paragraphs. This is the map a reader holds while reading the parts, and it is the difference between a sequence of pages and a book. Say where the argument turns, where it consolidates, and where it does something the reader will not expect from the opening.

Write this as continuous prose. A bulleted arc is a table of contents, and the book already has one of those.

How to read this one

Whether the parts follow the book's order or a better one. Reordering is allowed and is sometimes correct — but doing it silently is not, so if the order changed, give the mapping and the reason.

Note

A read it disposition is not a failure of the page. Some chapters cannot be transmitted this way — where the prose is the payload, where the effect comes from accumulation or voice. Saying so plainly, and saying what specifically does not survive, is the honest move. Concealing it is not.

Is the book still required?

Still required Answer this honestly and specifically. Name the chapters that did not transmit and say what is in them. If the answer is no — if these pages genuinely carry the book — say that too, because a reader deciding how to spend nine hours deserves a straight answer rather than a courtesy.

And point at the book. Every book index links to where it can be bought or borrowed; where a book is distributed free by its authors, link there and say so.
