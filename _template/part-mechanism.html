The ratchet

Technical exposition works by escalation, and so does good conceptual exposition. Transmit the escalation, not the endpoint. Do not skip to the final design: a reader handed the finished structure without first feeling the problem it solves has learned a fact, while a reader walked up the ratchet has learned why anyone would build such a thing — which is what lets them reason about the next system they meet. Books that do not ratchet explicitly still have one buried in them; find it and surface it.

  1. The obvious design, and why it was reasonable

    What we have
    State the current design so that it sounds reasonable — because it was reasonable. A ratchet that opens by sneering at the naive design teaches nothing, since the reader never believes anyone would have chosen it.
    What breaks
    The specific case that defeats it, with numbers or a concrete scenario. Not this is inefficient — how inefficient, on what input. A figure like 4 MB per process does work that the word "large" cannot.
    The fix
    What changes, and why that change and not another. The second half of that sentence is the one usually missing.
    What it costs
    Every fix buys something with something. Name the currency: memory, latency, I/O, bandwidth, complexity, generality, falsifiability.

  2. The second turn, forced by the first turn's cost

    What we have
    The design as the previous turn left it — including the debt it took on.
    What breaks
    Usually the cost from turn one, now dominant at scale.
    The fix
    The next structure, motivated rather than announced.
    What it costs
    And the currency it pays in, which is frequently what the following part is about.

Note

Where a book states a hardware assumption that no longer holds — seek times, cache sizes, a cost ratio that has since inverted — say so here, with the current figure. The mechanism usually survives; the arithmetic around it often does not.

One complete run, with real values

At least one complete run of the mechanism on a concrete input, every step shown, real values throughout. No step skipped as obvious — the skipped step is always the one the reader needed.

  1. Take the input 0x0040_1A3C and split it into the fields the figure names: field A is 0x001, field B is 0x001, offset is 0xA3C.
  2. Index the directory with field A. Entry 1 is valid and names chunk 0x07.
  3. Index that chunk with field B. Entry 1 holds 0x0092.
  4. Combine with the offset. The base is shifted by the same 12 bits the offset field occupies: (0x0092 << 12) | 0xA3C = 0x0009_2A3C. That is the result, and the arithmetic has been re-executed rather than proofread.

Keep a trace proportionate. Show the mechanism's characteristic operation rather than exhausting its state space: for a protocol with many message orderings, trace the common path in full and then name the interesting divergences rather than running each one.

The smallest version that shows the behaviour

Where the book uses code, it appears here as a minimal reimplementation — never a transcribed listing. Twenty readable lines beat eighty faithful ones. Highlighting is hand-applied or absent; there is no highlighter on this site to do it.

# A two-stage lookup, reduced to the part that matters.
def resolve(addr, directory, chunks):
    a      = (addr >> 22) & 0x3FF   # field A
    b      = (addr >> 12) & 0x3FF   # field B
    offset =  addr        & 0xFFF   # byte within the unit

    entry = directory[a]
    if not entry.valid:
        raise LookupError("no chunk for this range")

    base = chunks[entry.chunk][b].base    # the second access
    return (base << 12) | offset

The highlighted line is the one worth noticing: translation now costs a second memory access before the access the caller actually wanted, which is the cost the next turn of the ratchet has to pay down.

Voice

How the book handles technical material: whether it dialogues, whether it boxes its cruxes, whether it states the cost or leaves the reader to find it.

What this establishes, and what it asserts

For a mechanism part: what does the book state without justifying, and what is the justification actually? Books frequently assert a constant, a ratio, or a rule of thumb whose derivation is nowhere in the text.

Notes

Whether the described system still works this way; whether the hardware assumptions still hold; what the field concluded afterward; and the strongest objection the book does not answer.

What you carry forward

The mechanism in one sentence, the cost in one more, and the specific hook the next part hangs on — typically the cost this part took on.
