The Understudy

Readthroughs that transmit what a book actually contains — its arguments, its machinery, its evidence, its cases, its arc — at enough depth and completeness that reading the pages gives you what reading the book would have given you.

A summary tells you what a book is about. These pages tell you what is in it, which is roughly the difference between a menu and a meal. Nothing is asked of you except attention: there are no exercises, no drills, no flashcards, no progress tracking. Where a page shows work being done — a mechanism traced through a concrete input, an argument walked through a case — you watch. That is exposition, not assignment.

What each page is trying to earn is narrow and testable: that you could talk about the book for an hour with someone who read it cover to cover and never be caught out. How that is measured, and what these pages cannot do.

Cognitive science

Surfing Uncertainty

Andy Clark · 2016

A philosopher's synthesis arguing that one computational idea — that brains predict their own sensory stream and forward only the errors — accounts for perception, action, attention, imagination and a good deal of psychiatry, and that it vindicates rather than defeats the embodied view of mind.

10 parts 4 claim 4 mechanism 2 terrain 34,569 words ~198 min 11 full · 1 condensed · 2 folded

Prerequisites: none. No mathematics required — the formalism is carried in the traces.

Mostly not required The machinery, cases and evidence all transmit. What does not is the cumulative force of Part II: Clark's argument is inductive, and watching one mechanism absorb domain after domain across two hundred pages is an effect ten efficient parts cannot reproduce.
